# Safety tips

NosHen holds the buyer's money until they have the item. These habits keep you safe at every step.

## Codes are secret

- **Your release code is only in your NosHen app.** Give it to the seller only after you've checked the item, at the hand-over. Never send it by chat, SMS, WhatsApp or a call.
- **NosHen will never ask for your release code or your sign-in code.** Not by phone, email, WhatsApp or in the chat. Anyone who asks is trying to take your money.

## For buyers

- **Pay only into the deal account the app shows.** Never pay the seller directly, and never pay into another account they send you. Money paid outside NosHen isn't protected.
- **Check before you give your code.** The app shows three checks for what you're buying. If anything is wrong, keep your code and tap Report a problem. The money stays held.
- **Phones:** dial *#06# and check the IMEI matches. Make sure it isn't locked to someone else's account.
- **Hair and fashion:** check the length, size, colour and labels in good light.
- **Cars:** check the chassis number on the car matches the papers, take a test drive, and bring a mechanic if you can.

## For sellers

- **Wait for NosHen to show the money as held** before you hand anything over. A transfer alert, a screenshot or a call from "the bank" is not proof.
- **Take the code yourself.** At a meet-up, type the buyer's code into your NosHen app. The money is released when the code is correct.
- **Keep everything in the deal chat,** so a reviewer can see what was agreed if there's a problem.

## Meeting up

- Meet in a busy public place in daylight: a mall, a bank hall, or a police station car park.
- Bring someone with you, and tell someone where you're going.
- Don't carry cash. With NosHen you don't need to.
- If you feel unsafe, leave. You can cancel the deal before the hand-over, and the buyer gets their money back.

## Spotting a scam

- Someone asks you to pay or be paid outside NosHen. The app warns you when a chat message asks for this.
- A price that is far too low, or pressure to decide in minutes.
- A "NosHen agent" or "bank officer" asks for a code or for remote access to your phone.
- A buyer asks you to hand over before NosHen shows the money as held.

If something feels wrong, report it in the deal, or contact <mark>[SUPPORT EMAIL]</mark>. In an emergency, call the police on 112.
